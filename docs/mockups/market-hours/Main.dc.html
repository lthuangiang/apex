How to read this
  • Market volatility: how much the price jumps around within a minute. Darker cells mean that hour moved more than usual for that day.
  • Cost to make markets: when someone’s resting order gets filled, how far the price moves against them in the next 30 seconds, and how wide the gap between the best buy and sell price was.
  • {{readBots}}: what real-money bots paid for every $1M they traded in that hour. If only a few bots ran in an hour, its number is marked Thin sample and is less reliable.

Hours are shown in your time zone. These are past numbers, not a forecast.

How these numbers are calculated
1 · Market volatility
Data: SoDEX 1-minute price candles. For each minute we take the gap between its highest and lowest price, as a share of the price, in basis points (bp; 1 bp = 0.01%). For each hour of each day we take the middle value (median) of its 60 minutes and compare it with the median of the whole day. An hour counts as hot on a day when it is more than 2× that day’s median. Available since Oct 2, 2026.
2 · Cost to make markets
Data: public trades on SoDEX. After each trade we look at the price 30 seconds later: if it moved against the side whose order was resting in the book (the maker), that is the cost in bp. Spread is the gap between the best buy and sell price, counted in the smallest price steps (ticks). Both are medians per hour. Kept for 90 days.
3 · {{readBots}}
Data: the real fills of live Market Making and Volume Builder bots; practice bots are left out. Cost per $1M = (fees paid + price lost) ÷ volume traded × 1,000,000, for the fills in that hour. A bot-hour is one bot running for one hour, so 3 bots running for an hour is 3 bot-hours. Fewer than 3 bot-hours is a Thin sample. Available since Oct 20, 2026.
